## How each task is graded

Every task is marked on **reasoning**, not code or length. A task scores well
when it:

1. Answers the actual question (not a nearby, easier one).
2. Names what its answer **depends on** — the trade-off, the threat, the constraint.
3. Stays honest about what it does **not** yet settle.

Code, where a task asks for it, is a means to the reasoning — never the point.

## Format for every submission

- One or two pages (or the completed notebook + a short write-up).
- Plain language a teammate outside your track could follow.
- If you used AI, one closing line: which tool, and what for.
- Submit on Moodle by the unit deadline.

## The through-line

Keep a running document — your **project spine**. After each task, paste its
artifact under the matching decision heading. By unit 10 you are editing a draft
report, not starting one. See [`final-project.md`](final-project.md) and the
milestone checklist in the course materials.
