# Task 01 — Should you even run an experiment?

**Feeds:** Decision 1 — Should you experiment at all?
**Type:** Written / reasoning · no code
**Deliverable:** a one-page **decision card** for your project's core decision.

## Why this task

Before any statistics, an experiment has to earn the right to run: a result no
one would act on is wasted traffic, and some questions are already settled by
law, physics, or policy. This task fixes the *decision* your whole project will
be built on.

## What to do

Pick the real decision your group wants to test (a product, growth, pricing,
messaging, or policy change delivered through a digital channel). Then write a
decision card that answers:

1. **The decision.** What change is on the table, and who owns it?
2. **The pillar.** Is this descriptive, predictive, causal, or prescriptive?
   Only a causal question needs an experiment — say why yours is (or isn't).
3. **Would the result change anything?** Name the action each outcome triggers.
   If every outcome leads to the same action, stop here — you have no experiment.
4. **Is it already obvious?** Does law, physics, or policy pre-settle it? (If so,
   pick a different decision.)
5. **The three-period cost.** Planning, running (exposing users to a worse arm),
   and living with the result (the test you didn't run instead).
6. **Reversibility & stakes.** Can you roll back? Does being wrong cost more than
   finding out?

Close with one sentence: *"This decision needs an experiment because ___,"* or
*"This does not need an experiment because ___"* — and if the latter, choose
another decision and redo the card.

## Deliverable & format

One page. The six answers above plus the closing sentence. Plain language.

## How it feeds your final project

This card **is** Decision 1 of your report: the business choice, why
observational data isn't enough, and why an experiment is (or isn't) the
practical next step.

## Marked on

- Whether the decision is genuinely causal and genuinely reversible-worthy.
- Whether you named the action each result would trigger (not a vague "we'd learn").
- Whether the "depends on" is real — a stake, a constraint, an ethical line.
